Keep only the experts one subject uses, and Gemma 4 26B beats Gemma 4 12B, the dense model that fits the same mid-range machine. At 64 of 128 experts per layer (7.8 GB of weights) each subject's expert pack wins or ties 74% of blind-judged questions against the 12B, in all ten subjects, and computes about 4B parameters per token against the 12B's 12B.

The router sits at the centre. Each ring going outward is one of the 30 layers, holding its 128 experts in numbered slots. Bolts trace the experts a subject's text is routed to, layer by layer: its routing fingerprint. Compare what the full model uses with what a pack keeps, for one subject or up to three sharing one pack.

    Routing shares come from profiling each subject's text through the full model and counting the top-8 expert picks per token. An expert pack holds the shared core plus the subject's most used experts, up to the chosen size per layer. A shared pack serves several subjects by taking the experts they use most together, within the same size.

    Judged quality: 300 questions written from held-out Wikipedia articles, 30 per subject, each answered by that subject's own pack; Gemini 3.1 Pro compared each pair blind in both orders, and a win counts only when both orders agree. Per-subject figures rest on 30 questions each, so treat single subjects as rough. Loss is extra next-token loss on held-out text versus the full model, measured at 51 per layer only; for two or three subjects it is the average over random sets of that size. Memory is 4-bit weights held in memory, before activations. The bolts between layers are drawn for legibility: the router does not pass tokens from one particular expert to another.
