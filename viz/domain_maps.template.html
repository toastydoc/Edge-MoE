Judged blind against the full model on 300 questions, a domain's own pack wins or ties 37% of the time (one pack for all ten: 28%); most losses are factual errors. The other experts stay in RAM or on disk only if you want to switch packs; a device for one domain needs just the shared core and its pack.

    Routing shares come from profiling each domain's text through the full model and counting the top-8 expert picks per token. An expert pack holds a domain's most used experts outside the shared core, up to 51 per layer in total. A shared pack serves several domains by taking the experts they use most together, within the same budget of 51 per layer.

    Quality cost is extra next-token loss on each domain's held-out Wikipedia text versus the full model; for two or three domains it is the average measured over random sets of that size, not for this exact combination. Memory is peak GPU memory in use. The bolts between layers are drawn for legibility: the router does not pass tokens from one particular expert to another.
